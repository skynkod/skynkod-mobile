## Acne and pores

### Blackheads and whiteheads

- **What you may notice:** Small dark dots on the nose, chin or forehead · Little skin-coloured or white bumps that don’t hurt
- **Skincare can:** Salicylic acid (BHA) unclogs pores from the inside · A retinoid (retinol, adapalene) stops new plugs forming · Niacinamide helps balance oil
- **Skincare can’t:** Deep, stubborn plugs sometimes need a professional to remove them
- **May be prescribed:** Tretinoin or stronger adapalene (Prescription retinoids that clear clogged pores more strongly)
- **Clinic treatments:** Professional extraction (Clears stubborn plugs safely, without marks; recovery: None) · Salicylic acid peel (Deep-cleans pores and smooths texture; recovery: 1 to 3 days of light peeling)
- **Dermatologist (check):** See a dermatologist if nothing improves after 3 months of a steady routine.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, comedonal acne](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/comedonal-acne) · [NHS, Acne](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/acne/)

### Red, inflamed spots

- **What you may notice:** Red, tender bumps · Spots with a white or yellow head
- **Skincare can:** Benzoyl peroxide reduces acne bacteria · Azelaic acid calms redness and helps clear spots · Salicylic acid and niacinamide support the routine · Not picking prevents marks and scars
- **Skincare can’t:** Widespread, painful acne rarely clears with skincare alone
- **May be prescribed:** A retinoid with benzoyl peroxide (Prescription creams that treat several causes at once) · Clindamycin with benzoyl peroxide (A topical antibiotic, always paired with benzoyl peroxide) · Doxycycline or lymecycline (Oral antibiotics for a few months for moderate acne)
- **Clinic treatments:** Blue light or LED therapy (Can calm active spots alongside treatment; recovery: None) · Chemical peels (Help clear spots and fade marks; recovery: 2 to 5 days)
- **Dermatologist (soon):** See a dermatologist if spots leave marks or scars, or don’t improve after 3 months.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, acne](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/acne) · [NHS, Acne](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/acne/)

### Cystic acne

- **What you may notice:** Deep, painful lumps under the skin · Spots that last for weeks and leave marks
- **Skincare can:** A gentle routine keeps the skin calm while the treatment works · Benzoyl peroxide can help around the cysts · Never squeeze them: it spreads inflammation and scars
- **Skincare can’t:** Cysts are too deep for creams to reach: they need a medical treatment
- **May be prescribed:** Isotretinoin (The most effective treatment for severe acne, with strict medical follow-up (never during pregnancy)) · Oral antibiotics (For a few months, with a topical treatment) · Hormonal treatment (For women: a combined pill or spironolactone, when acne is hormonal)
- **Clinic treatments:** Cortisone injection into a cyst (Done by a doctor, it can flatten a painful cyst within days; recovery: None)
- **Dermatologist (soon):** See a dermatologist soon: cystic acne can leave lasting scars, and effective treatments exist.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, nodulocystic acne](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/nodulocystic-acne) · [DermNet, isotretinoin](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/isotretinoin)

### Hormonal acne

- **What you may notice:** Spots along the jaw and chin · Breakouts that come back around your period
- **Skincare can:** Azelaic acid, benzoyl peroxide and salicylic acid treat the spots · A retinoid helps prevent new ones · Your journal can show if they follow your cycle
- **Skincare can’t:** Skincare can’t change hormones: it treats the spots, not their cause
- **May be prescribed:** A combined contraceptive pill (Some pills reduce hormonal acne) · Spironolactone (A tablet that calms the hormone effect on oil glands (women only, not in pregnancy)) · A prescription retinoid (To prevent new spots)
- **Clinic treatments:** Chemical peels (Help with spots and the marks they leave; recovery: 2 to 5 days)
- **Dermatologist (check):** See a dermatologist or a gynaecologist, especially with irregular periods or extra hair growth.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, acne](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/acne) · [NHS, Acne](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/acne/)

### Fungal acne

- **What you may notice:** Tiny bumps, all the same size · They itch, and get worse with heat, sweat or rich creams
- **Skincare can:** Skip oils and rich creams that feed the yeast · Zinc pyrithione cleansers calm it · A ketoconazole 2% shampoo, used as a 5-minute face mask 2 or 3 times a week, often helps (ask your pharmacist or dermatologist)
- **Skincare can’t:** Classic acne treatments and antibiotics don’t work on it, and can make it worse
- **May be prescribed:** Ketoconazole cream (An antifungal cream applied on the bumps) · Oral antifungals (Itraconazole or fluconazole for a short course, when it’s widespread)
- **Dermatologist (check):** See a dermatologist to confirm it’s fungal: it looks like acne but needs a different treatment.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, malassezia folliculitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/malassezia-folliculitis) · [DermNet, ketoconazole](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/ketoconazole)

### Oily skin

- **What you may notice:** Shiny by midday · Make-up slides off
- **Skincare can:** A gentle cleanser twice a day, never a harsh one · Niacinamide and zinc help balance oil · A light gel moisturizer: dry skin makes more oil
- **Skincare can’t:** Oil production is mostly genetic and hormonal: skincare manages it, it doesn’t stop it
- **May be prescribed:** Low-dose isotretinoin or hormonal treatment (Sometimes considered for very oily skin, by a dermatologist)
- **Clinic treatments:** Chemical peels (Temporarily reduce shine and refine texture; recovery: 1 to 3 days)
- **Dermatologist (check):** See a dermatologist if oiliness comes with acne that doesn’t improve.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, seborrhoea](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/seborrhoea)

### Enlarged pores

- **What you may notice:** Visible pores on the nose and cheeks
- **Skincare can:** Salicylic acid keeps pores clear so they look smaller · A retinoid and niacinamide refine texture · Sunscreen: sun damage loosens the skin around pores
- **Skincare can’t:** Pores can’t be closed or shrunk for good: skincare makes them look smaller
- **May be prescribed:** Prescription retinoid (Refines texture over months)
- **Clinic treatments:** Laser resurfacing (Tightens the skin around pores; recovery: 3 to 10 days) · Microneedling (Stimulates collagen to refine texture; recovery: 1 to 3 days of redness)
- **Sources:** [DermNet, enlarged pores](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/enlarged-pores)

### Sebaceous filaments

- **What you may notice:** Grey or beige dots on the nose that look like blackheads · They come back a few days after squeezing
- **Skincare can:** Salicylic acid and clay masks make them less visible · Stop squeezing: it stretches pores
- **Skincare can’t:** They’re normal: they always fill back up
- **Clinic treatments:** Professional facial with extraction (Clears them for a while; recovery: None)
- **Sources:** [DermNet, enlarged pores](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/enlarged-pores)

### Milia

- **What you may notice:** Tiny, hard, white pearls · Often around the eyes or on the cheeks
- **Skincare can:** A gentle retinoid or exfoliating acid can help new ones clear · Lighter eye creams
- **Skincare can’t:** They’re under a thin layer of skin: never try to squeeze them
- **May be prescribed:** Topical retinoid (Can help them come out over weeks)
- **Clinic treatments:** Professional extraction (A tiny opening, then the pearl comes out; recovery: None to 2 days)
- **Dermatologist (check):** See a dermatologist or a trained aesthetician to remove them safely.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, milium](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/milium)

### Sebaceous hyperplasia

- **What you may notice:** Small yellowish bumps with a dip in the middle · Usually on the forehead or cheeks, after 30
- **Skincare can:** A retinoid may make them less visible
- **Skincare can’t:** Skincare doesn’t remove them
- **May be prescribed:** Topical retinoid (Can flatten them a little)
- **Clinic treatments:** Electrocautery or laser (Removes them one by one; recovery: 5 to 10 days of small scabs)
- **Dermatologist (check):** See a dermatologist to confirm: some other bumps can look similar and need checking.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, sebaceous hyperplasia](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/sebaceous-hyperplasia)

## Redness and reactive skin

### Rosacea

- **What you may notice:** Redness on the cheeks and nose that comes and goes · Flushing with heat, spicy food, alcohol or stress · Sometimes small spots without blackheads
- **Skincare can:** Azelaic acid and niacinamide calm redness · A gentle, fragrance-free routine · Mineral sunscreen every day · Your journal can find your triggers
- **Skincare can’t:** Visible small vessels don’t go away with creams · Rosacea is long-term: skincare controls it, it doesn’t cure it
- **May be prescribed:** Metronidazole gel or cream (e.g. Rozex) (Calms the spots and redness of rosacea) · Ivermectin cream (Treats the bumps and spots of rosacea) · Azelaic acid 15% (A stronger, prescription form) · Brimonidine gel (Reduces redness for a few hours) · Low-dose doxycycline (Oral, for spots that keep coming back)
- **Clinic treatments:** Vascular laser or IPL (Reduces lasting redness and visible vessels; recovery: 2 to 7 days of mild redness)
- **Dermatologist (check):** See a dermatologist to confirm rosacea: prescription treatments work much better than skincare alone.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, rosacea](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/rosacea) · [NHS, Rosacea](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/rosacea/) · [DermNet, metronidazole](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/metronidazole) · [DermNet, ivermectin](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/ivermectin)

### Visible small vessels

- **What you may notice:** Thin red lines on the cheeks or around the nose
- **Skincare can:** Sunscreen and gentle care stop them getting worse · Avoid very hot water and harsh scrubs
- **Skincare can’t:** Creams can’t remove vessels that are already visible
- **Clinic treatments:** Vascular laser or IPL (Closes the small vessels; recovery: 2 to 7 days)
- **Dermatologist (check):** A dermatologist can check for rosacea, which often causes them.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, telangiectasia](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/telangiectasia)

### Sensitive skin

- **What you may notice:** Stinging, burning or itching with many products · Redness that comes quickly
- **Skincare can:** A short routine: gentle cleanser, moisturizer, sunscreen · Ceramides and panthenol rebuild the barrier · Fragrance-free products, one new product at a time
- **Skincare can’t:** If it stays sensitive, there may be a condition behind it (rosacea, eczema, an allergy)
- **Clinic treatments:** Avoid strong peels and lasers (Until your skin is calm and a dermatologist agrees; recovery: —)
- **Dermatologist (check):** See a dermatologist if it doesn’t settle: a patch test can find an allergy.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, sensitive skin](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/sensitive-skin)

### Perioral dermatitis

- **What you may notice:** Small red bumps around the mouth or nose · They sting rather than itch, and spare a thin line right next to the lips
- **Skincare can:** Stop rich creams, and never use steroid creams on it · A very gentle, minimal routine · Azelaic acid can help
- **Skincare can’t:** It usually needs a prescription treatment to clear
- **May be prescribed:** Metronidazole gel (A first-line cream) · Doxycycline or lymecycline (Oral, for a few weeks when it’s widespread)
- **Dermatologist (soon):** See a dermatologist: steroid creams make it worse, and the right treatment clears it.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, periorificial dermatitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/periorificial-dermatitis)

### Seborrheic dermatitis

- **What you may notice:** Red, flaky patches with greasy, yellowish scales · Around the nose, eyebrows and hairline
- **Skincare can:** Zinc pyrithione cleansers · A ketoconazole shampoo used briefly on the patches (ask your pharmacist) · A light, oil-free moisturizer
- **Skincare can’t:** It comes and goes: skincare controls flares, it doesn’t cure it
- **May be prescribed:** Ketoconazole cream (Treats the yeast involved) · A short, mild steroid or a calcineurin inhibitor (For flares, only as prescribed)
- **Dermatologist (check):** See a dermatologist to confirm it and get a treatment for flares.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, seborrhoeic dermatitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/seborrhoeic-dermatitis) · [DermNet, ketoconazole](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/ketoconazole)

### Eczema

- **What you may notice:** Dry, itchy, rough patches · Often on the eyelids, around the mouth or the neck
- **Skincare can:** A rich, fragrance-free moisturizer twice a day · Ceramides, glycerin and shea · Lukewarm water, gentle cleanser, nothing that stings
- **Skincare can’t:** Flares need medical treatment: moisturizer alone won’t stop them
- **May be prescribed:** Topical steroids (Short courses to calm flares, as prescribed) · Tacrolimus or pimecrolimus (Steroid-free creams for the face and eyelids)
- **Dermatologist (soon):** See a doctor or dermatologist for flares, and if the skin weeps, crusts or spreads.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, atopic dermatitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/atopic-dermatitis) · [DermNet, emollients and moisturisers](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/emollients-and-moisturisers)

### Psoriasis on the face

- **What you may notice:** Well-defined red patches with silvery scales · Often on the hairline, eyebrows or around the nose
- **Skincare can:** Gentle moisturizers soften scales · Avoid scratching and harsh products
- **Skincare can’t:** Psoriasis needs medical treatment: skincare only supports it
- **May be prescribed:** Mild topical steroids (Short courses for the face) · Calcineurin inhibitors or vitamin D creams (Longer-term options for facial skin) · Systemic treatments (For widespread psoriasis)
- **Dermatologist (soon):** See a dermatologist: psoriasis is treated medically, and can involve joints too.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, facial psoriasis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/facial-psoriasis)

### Irritation or allergy to a product

- **What you may notice:** Redness, stinging or itching after a new product · It appears where you applied it
- **Skincare can:** Stop the new product right away · Back to basics: gentle cleanser and a soothing moisturizer · A cool compress calms it
- **Skincare can’t:** Skincare can’t tell you which ingredient you’re allergic to
- **May be prescribed:** A short course of mild steroid (To calm a strong reaction, as prescribed)
- **Clinic treatments:** Patch testing (Done by a dermatologist to find the ingredient you react to; recovery: None)
- **Dermatologist (urgent):** If your face, lips or eyes swell, or you have trouble breathing, get medical help right away. Otherwise, see a dermatologist for patch testing if it keeps happening.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, contact dermatitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/contact-dermatitis) · [DermNet, irritant contact dermatitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/irritant-contact-dermatitis)

### Red marks after spots

- **What you may notice:** Pink or red flat marks where spots used to be
- **Skincare can:** Sunscreen every day so they fade faster · Niacinamide, azelaic acid and centella · Patience: they often fade over months
- **Skincare can’t:** Exfoliating harder doesn’t speed them up: they’re small vessels, not pigment
- **Clinic treatments:** Vascular laser or IPL (Fades lasting red marks; recovery: 2 to 7 days)
- **Sources:** [DermNet, acne scarring](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/acne-scarring)

## Dark spots

### Melasma

- **What you may notice:** Brown patches on the cheeks, forehead or upper lip · Darker in summer, sometimes after pregnancy or the pill
- **Skincare can:** A broad-spectrum, tinted sunscreen every day (it also blocks visible light) · Azelaic acid, tranexamic acid, niacinamide, vitamin C · Gentle care: irritation makes it darker
- **Skincare can’t:** Melasma is long-term and often comes back: it’s controlled, rarely cured
- **May be prescribed:** Hydroquinone or a triple combination cream (Prescription lighteners, for limited periods) · Oral tranexamic acid (Sometimes prescribed for stubborn melasma)
- **Clinic treatments:** Gentle chemical peels (Can help alongside treatment; recovery: 2 to 5 days)
- **Dermatologist (check):** See a dermatologist before any laser: lasers can make melasma worse if not chosen carefully.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, melasma](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/melasma) · [DermNet, tranexamic acid](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/tranexamic-acid) · [DermNet, hydroquinone](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/hydroquinone)

### Dark marks after spots

- **What you may notice:** Brown or dark marks where spots or irritation were
- **Skincare can:** Sunscreen every day: the sun keeps them dark · Niacinamide, azelaic acid, vitamin C and a retinoid fade them · Treat the spots to stop new marks
- **Skincare can’t:** They fade slowly, over months
- **May be prescribed:** Hydroquinone or tretinoin (Prescription options for stubborn marks)
- **Clinic treatments:** Chemical peels (Speed up fading; recovery: 2 to 5 days)
- **Sources:** [DermNet, postinflammatory hyperpigmentation](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/postinflammatory-hyperpigmentation)

### Sun spots

- **What you may notice:** Flat brown spots on sun-exposed areas · They appear with age and sun
- **Skincare can:** Sunscreen every day stops new ones · Vitamin C and a retinoid can lighten them a little
- **Skincare can’t:** Creams rarely remove them completely
- **Clinic treatments:** IPL or pigment laser (Removes sun spots; recovery: 5 to 10 days of darker spots that flake off) · Cryotherapy (Freezes a single spot; recovery: A few days)
- **Dermatologist (urgent):** Show any spot that changes, grows, bleeds or looks different from the others to a dermatologist quickly.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, solar lentigo](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/solar-lentigo)

## Dryness and lips

### Dry skin

- **What you may notice:** Tight, rough, sometimes flaky · Feels better right after moisturizer
- **Skincare can:** Ceramides, glycerin, shea and urea · A cream cleanser and lukewarm water · Moisturizer on damp skin
- **Skincare can’t:** If it itches, cracks or turns red, it may be eczema
- **Dermatologist (check):** See a dermatologist if it itches, cracks or doesn’t improve with a good moisturizer.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, dry skin](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/dry-skin) · [DermNet, emollients and moisturisers](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/emollients-and-moisturisers)

### Dehydrated skin

- **What you may notice:** Tight and dull, even if it’s oily · Fine lines that show more on some days
- **Skincare can:** Hyaluronic acid and glycerin draw water in · A moisturizer on top keeps it there · Fewer harsh cleansers and drying alcohol
- **Skincare can’t:** It’s a state, not a skin type: it improves fast once the routine is gentle
- **Sources:** [DermNet, dry skin](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/dry-skin)

### Chapped lips

- **What you may notice:** Dry, cracked, peeling lips
- **Skincare can:** A thick balm several times a day, and a lip mask at night · An SPF lip balm outside · Stop licking them
- **Skincare can’t:** If they stay cracked for weeks, something else may be going on
- **Dermatologist (check):** See a dermatologist if they stay rough or scaly for weeks, especially on the lower lip.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, cheilitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/cheilitis) · [DermNet, actinic cheilitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/actinic-cheilitis)

### Cracked mouth corners

- **What you may notice:** Sore, red cracks at the corners of the mouth
- **Skincare can:** A barrier ointment at the corners
- **Skincare can’t:** It often involves yeast or bacteria: it needs the right cream
- **May be prescribed:** An antifungal or antibacterial cream (Sometimes with a mild steroid, as prescribed)
- **Dermatologist (check):** Ask a pharmacist or doctor: they may also check your iron and vitamins.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, angular cheilitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/angular-cheilitis)

## Lines, firmness and glow

### Fine lines

- **What you may notice:** Thin lines around the eyes or on the forehead · More visible when skin is dry
- **Skincare can:** A retinoid smooths lines over months · Sunscreen every day: the sun is the first cause · Peptides, hyaluronic acid and vitamin C
- **Skincare can’t:** Expression lines from muscle movement come back with every smile or frown
- **May be prescribed:** Tretinoin (A prescription retinoid with strong evidence for fine lines)
- **Clinic treatments:** Botulinum toxin (Softens expression lines (frown, crow’s feet) for 3 to 4 months; recovery: None) · Light peels or microneedling (Smooth texture; recovery: 1 to 3 days)
- **Sources:** [DermNet, ageing skin](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/ageing-skin) · [DermNet, botulinum toxin](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/botulinum-toxin)

### Deep wrinkles

- **What you may notice:** Lines that stay even when your face is relaxed
- **Skincare can:** A retinoid and sunscreen improve skin quality slowly
- **Skincare can’t:** Creams don’t erase deep wrinkles
- **May be prescribed:** Tretinoin (Improves skin quality over many months)
- **Clinic treatments:** Botulinum toxin (Relaxes the muscles that crease the skin; recovery: None) · Fillers (Fill folds and deep lines; recovery: 2 to 7 days of possible swelling) · Laser resurfacing (Remodels the skin in depth; recovery: 5 to 14 days)
- **Dermatologist (check):** A dermatologist or aesthetic doctor can tell you which treatment suits your face.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, ageing skin](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/ageing-skin) · [DermNet, fillers](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/fillers) · [DermNet, laser resurfacing](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/laser-resurfacing)

### Sagging skin

- **What you may notice:** Skin feels looser · Heaviness on the lower face or jowls
- **Skincare can:** Retinoids support collagen and texture · Peptides play a supporting role · Moisturizer plumps temporarily · Sunscreen stops UV breaking collagen down
- **Skincare can’t:** Skincare won’t lift loose skin · If volume loss or bone changes are part of it, creams can’t fix that
- **May be prescribed:** Tretinoin (Improves skin quality, not the lift)
- **Clinic treatments:** Radiofrequency or ultrasound tightening (Heats deep layers to tighten the skin over months; recovery: None to a few days) · Radiofrequency microneedling (Tightens and improves texture; recovery: 2 to 5 days) · Fillers or biostimulators (When volume loss is part of it; recovery: 2 to 7 days)
- **Dermatologist (check):** An aesthetic doctor can tell you whether it’s mostly loose skin, lost volume or both, and what will actually help. Surgery is the option for advanced sagging.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, ageing skin](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/ageing-skin) · [DermNet, fillers](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/fillers)

### Loss of volume

- **What you may notice:** Hollow cheeks or temples · Your face looks thinner or more tired
- **Skincare can:** Hydration plumps the skin a little, for a few hours
- **Skincare can’t:** No cream can replace lost facial volume
- **Clinic treatments:** Hyaluronic acid fillers (Restore volume where it was lost; recovery: 2 to 7 days of possible swelling) · Biostimulators (Help your skin rebuild volume gradually; recovery: A few days) · Avoid fat-reducing treatments (When the face is already hollow, they can make it look thinner; recovery: —)
- **Dermatologist (check):** An aesthetic doctor can assess your face’s volume and structure before any treatment.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, fillers](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/fillers) · [DermNet, ageing skin](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/ageing-skin)

### Uneven texture

- **What you may notice:** Rough or bumpy skin · Make-up doesn’t sit smoothly
- **Skincare can:** Gentle exfoliating acids (AHA, PHA) · A retinoid · Daily moisturizer and sunscreen
- **Skincare can’t:** Scars and deep texture need clinic treatments
- **May be prescribed:** Tretinoin (Smooths texture over months)
- **Clinic treatments:** Chemical peels (Smooth the surface; recovery: 2 to 5 days) · Microneedling (Refines texture with collagen; recovery: 1 to 3 days)
- **Sources:** [DermNet, chemical peels](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/chemical-peels) · [DermNet, skin needling](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/skin-needling)

### Dull skin

- **What you may notice:** Skin looks tired and lacks glow
- **Skincare can:** Vitamin C and niacinamide · Gentle exfoliation once or twice a week · Hydration, sleep and sunscreen
- **Skincare can’t:** Glow also depends on sleep, stress and health
- **Clinic treatments:** Light peels or a hydrating facial (A quick, temporary boost; recovery: None to 2 days)
- **Sources:** [DermNet, chemical peels](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/chemical-peels)

## Eyes

### Dark circles

- **What you may notice:** Brown shadows (pigment) · Blue or purple shade (thin skin, vessels) · A hollow that casts a shadow
- **Skincare can:** For brown circles: sunscreen, vitamin C, niacinamide · For thin skin: a retinoid eye cream and peptides · Sleep, allergies and rubbing play a big part
- **Skincare can’t:** Hollows and shadows from your face shape don’t respond to creams
- **Clinic treatments:** Under-eye fillers (For hollows, done by an experienced doctor; recovery: 3 to 10 days of possible swelling) · Peels or laser (For pigmented circles; recovery: 2 to 7 days)
- **Dermatologist (check):** A dermatologist can tell you which type you have, and what will really help.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, dark circles under the eyes](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/dark-circles-under-the-eyes)

### Puffy eyes

- **What you may notice:** Swelling under the eyes, worse in the morning
- **Skincare can:** A cool compress and caffeine eye products help for a few hours · Sleep, less salt and alcohol in the evening
- **Skincare can’t:** Permanent under-eye bags are fat pads: creams can’t remove them
- **Clinic treatments:** Eyelid surgery (The only lasting option for permanent bags; recovery: 1 to 2 weeks)
- **Dermatologist (urgent):** See a doctor if the swelling is sudden, on one side only, or comes with redness or itching.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, dark circles under the eyes](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/dark-circles-under-the-eyes)

## Scars

### Acne scars

- **What you may notice:** Small dips or pits in the skin after acne
- **Skincare can:** A retinoid and sunscreen improve skin quality a little · Treating active acne prevents new scars
- **Skincare can’t:** Creams can’t fill in scars
- **Clinic treatments:** Microneedling (Rebuilds collagen under shallow scars; recovery: 1 to 3 days) · Subcision (Releases scars that pull the skin down; recovery: A few days of bruising) · Laser resurfacing (Smooths deeper scars; recovery: 5 to 14 days) · Peels or fillers (For some scar types; recovery: 2 to 7 days)
- **Dermatologist (check):** A dermatologist can match the treatment to your type of scars, often combining several.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, acne scarring](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/acne-scarring) · [DermNet, subcision](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/subcision) · [DermNet, skin needling](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/skin-needling)

### Raised scars

- **What you may notice:** Firm, raised scars, sometimes itchy
- **Skincare can:** Silicone gel or sheets flatten them over months · Sunscreen keeps them from darkening
- **Skincare can’t:** Large or growing scars need medical treatment
- **May be prescribed:** Steroid injections (Flatten raised scars, by a dermatologist)
- **Clinic treatments:** Laser (Reduces redness and thickness; recovery: A few days)
- **Dermatologist (check):** See a dermatologist if a scar keeps growing beyond the original wound.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, keloid and hypertrophic scar](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/keloid-and-hypertrophic-scar)

## Sun

### Sunburn

- **What you may notice:** Red, hot, painful skin after the sun
- **Skincare can:** Cool water, a gentle soothing moisturizer (aloe, panthenol) · Pause all actives until it heals · Stay out of the sun
- **Skincare can’t:** Skincare doesn’t undo the damage: prevention is everything
- **Dermatologist (urgent):** See a doctor if you have large blisters, fever, chills or feel unwell. A pharmacist can advise on pain relief.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, sunburn](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/sunburn)

### Sun-damaged skin

- **What you may notice:** Spots, uneven tone, lines and rough patches on sun-exposed skin
- **Skincare can:** Sunscreen every day, a retinoid and vitamin C
- **Skincare can’t:** Rough, scaly patches that don’t heal must be checked by a doctor
- **May be prescribed:** Tretinoin (Repairs sun damage over months)
- **Clinic treatments:** IPL, laser or peels (Improve spots and tone; recovery: 5 to 10 days) · Photodynamic therapy (Treats sun-damaged patches, by a dermatologist; recovery: A week)
- **Dermatologist (soon):** See a dermatologist for a skin check, especially for rough patches or spots that change.
- **Sources:** [DermNet, ageing skin](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/ageing-skin) · [DermNet, photodynamic therapy](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/photodynamic-therapy)
